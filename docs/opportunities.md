# Choosing what to build

Decision record · 27 November 2026. These ten proposals were assessed for a maintainer already working on Burmese localization, subtitle and narration workflows, automated silence cutting, Python, FFmpeg, and low-cost infrastructure. A project that can run on a laptop, produce an inspectable result and benefit a second language community fits those skills. No personal details, customer interviews or revenue assumptions are needed for this assessment.

## Evidence of meaningful use

* YouTube accepts basic UTF-8 SRT, putting the output directly into an established publishing workflow. W3C documents who needs synchronized captions and explains that authoring includes transcription and timestamping. These establish format reach and accessibility importance. [YouTube's supported files](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2734698?hl=en) · [W3C caption guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/media/av/captions/)
* LosslessCut's v3.70.2 release API showed **175,628 downloads of the Windows x64 archive** and **28,732 of the Apple Silicon disk image** when checked. These count downloads of specific files, including repeat downloads. They establish distribution of a compatible editor. They cannot count users who need external subtitle review. [Release](https://github.com/mifi/lossless-cut/releases/tag/v3.70.2) · [Recheck asset counters](https://api.github.com/repos/mifi/lossless-cut/releases/tags/v3.70.2)
* In 2022 a LosslessCut user described incorrect external subtitle timing after cutting and reported manual steps for merged clips. The maintainer explained the workflow constraint at the time. This documents one real failure in an older version. It does not establish a current bug or its frequency. [Discussion and workaround](https://github.com/mifi/lossless-cut/discussions/1200)
* Current alternatives are substantial. Auto-editor maps embedded subtitle streams while rendering; ffsubsync aligns timestamps against reference material; Cuescribe explicitly supports external SRT/VTT cut lists, clips and joined recuts. A basic retimer alone would duplicate existing work. The proposed contribution is an offline, scriptable record of every cue, a conservative default for cut dialogue, and a review exit code. We have no measured demand for this combination yet. [Auto-editor](https://auto-editor.com/docs/v3) · [ffsubsync](https://github.com/smacke/ffsubsync) · [Cuescribe](https://cuescribe.app/split-subtitles)

## Weighted evaluation

Each criterion receives 0–10. Multiply each rating by its percentage weight and divide the sum by 10. Importance concerns harm or work avoided; usefulness measures how many different workflows could benefit. Demand measures evidence of an inadequately served task; it is intentionally lower where alternatives already address it. Scores are judgments, not measured market shares. A 9 means strong fit or evidence, 7 means plausible with constraints and 5 means substantial uncertainty or overlap.

## Candidate briefs

| Project, intended users and problem | Proposed contribution and impact | Alternatives, evidence and uncertainty | Complexity, main risk and license |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **SpliceLedger** · Captioners, educators and video editors must revisit translated lines after cuts. | Apply a retained-range list to SRT. Give a reviewer the old and new time of every affected line. Could preserve paid translation work and reduce missed cut dialogue. | W3C, YouTube, LosslessCut, auto-editor and Cuescribe above support importance and feasibility. A human-review handoff differentiates it. Frequency of reuse is unmeasured. | Low–medium. Inaccurate edit points misplace captions, and timing cannot recover words. **Apache-2.0** for tool and pipeline integrations. |
| **ScriptWitness** · Burmese ASR maintainers struggle to tell which user-facing errors changed between model versions. | Compare two transcripts against consented references. Surface changed passages and reviewer notes alongside character error rate. Could make model regressions visible. | [JiWER](https://github.com/jitsi/jiwer) provides error measures; [Myanmar Tools](https://github.com/google/myanmar-tools) demonstrates encoding complications. Excellent maintainer fit. Review labor, text normalization choices and data access lower feasibility and sustainability. | Medium–high. A narrow corpus can reward a model that fails other speakers. **Apache-2.0** for code; license each corpus separately. |
| **CueParity** · Translators can lose an entire caption or sound description in a language handoff. | Compare source and translated track coverage and show time gaps for review, without judging translation quality. Could catch omitted accessible content. | [Weblate](https://docs.weblate.org/en/latest/user/checks.html) already runs translation QA; Subtitle Edit provides a mature editing environment. Easy integration and strong fit, but legitimate cue splits produce false positives. | Medium. Reviewer fatigue. **MPL-2.0** to share changes to matching rules. |
| **AudioRights Map** · Volunteer audio archives often need to preserve attribution, provenance and permission separately for each recording. | Package source, permission evidence location and permitted uses with each file; flag missing records before export. Could make handovers easier. | [SPDX](https://spdx.dev/learn/overview/) describes license metadata, [BagIt](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8493) packages files. Applying both to archive permissions needs institutional partners; metadata cannot establish ownership. | Medium–high. False assurance about permission. **Apache-2.0** for integrations; records retain their own rights terms. |
| **LessonCrate** · Teachers need to move captioned lessons between intermittently connected devices. | Build a USB-ready lesson with a transcript, language selection and checksums. Could let small classes share accessible teaching material. | [Kolibri](https://learningequality.org/kolibri/) already serves offline learning; [BagIt](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8493) verifies file transfers. Practical need, but another distribution format would have high coordination cost. | Medium. Device and player compatibility. **MPL-2.0** to share player improvements. |
| **TakeResume** · Small AI studios lose work when GPU sessions expire mid-job. | Checkpoint numbered audio chunks and rerun only missing verified outputs. Could reduce duplicate computation. | [ZeroGPU](https://huggingface.co/docs/hub/spaces-zerogpu) documents time budgets; [Prefect](https://docs.prefect.io/) supplies general orchestration. Strong personal fit; provider churn and cross-chunk voice continuity limit a generic adapter. | High. Visible seams and changing APIs. **Apache-2.0** for adapters. |
| **RestoreRehearsal** · Small self-hosters have backups but may never have restored a complete application. | Run a read-only backup drill into an isolated directory and verify app-level fixtures. Could reveal backups that cannot bring a service back. | [restic](https://restic.readthedocs.io/en/stable/045_working_with_repos.html) already checks repository data. Useful infrastructure problem; application-specific verification limits reuse. | High. Handling untrusted backup content and avoiding destructive targets. **Apache-2.0** for backup-system integrations. |
| **MixReceipt** · Volunteer narrators need to know why two encodes of one talk sound different. | Record loudness measurements, parameters and tool versions before and after mastering. Could improve repeatability and handoffs. | [FFmpeg filters](https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html) and [ffmpeg-normalize](https://github.com/slhck/ffmpeg-normalize) already cover measurement and normalization. Feasible to maintain, with a small differentiation margin. | Low–medium. Loudness metrics miss clipping and intelligibility issues. **MIT** for easy embedding. |
| **ShareScrub** · Creators may share media or project bundles with unintended metadata or credentials. | List exposed metadata and likely secret fields locally, with a human preview before making an export copy. Could prevent accidental disclosures. | [Gitleaks](https://github.com/gitleaks/gitleaks) already scans for secrets. [ffprobe](https://ffmpeg.org/ffprobe.html) exposes media metadata. Broad importance, but coverage is hard to define and missed data has real consequences. | High. Users could over-trust a clean report. **MPL-2.0** for shared detection rules. |
| **CostFrame** · Small teams comparing render providers cannot tell which finished job consumed the most money. | Combine optional receipts with measured run time and successful output minutes. Could improve provider decisions. | [OpenCost](https://opencost.io/docs/) models infrastructure costs; [ZeroGPU](https://huggingface.co/docs/hub/spaces-zerogpu) documents a different quota model. Good developer fit. Marketing prices, retries and failed jobs make estimates fragile. | Medium–high. Stale rates and misleading precision. **Apache-2.0** for provider adapters. |

## Why build the first one?

SpliceLedger can be checked against an independent clock and real cut audio without collecting media, training a model or recruiting a rights-cleared corpus. Its output works in existing subtitle tools. ScriptWitness could address a deeper language need but depends on reviewers and a representative, permissioned evaluation set. Under these weights, reducing SpliceLedger's demand rating from 7 to 4 would take it from 87 to 82.5, below ScriptWitness at 83. The recommendation should change if editors rarely encounter partial cues or already resolve them cheaply.

**Adoption test.** Recruit three consenting editors who already exchange external captions. For two comparable edits per editor, measure manual review time, missed captions, captions incorrectly retained, and whether the editor chooses the tool again. Never collect dialogue by default. If participants mostly need native editor export or see little benefit, prioritize an upstream adapter or contribute the review policy to an established editor. No third-party pilots, users, revenue or adoption are claimed at launch.
